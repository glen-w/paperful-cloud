What I send back

You already have the pile. I run the fill. You get a bibliography and a miss list back, in the reference manager you already use. RIS, BibTeX, CSV, EndNote XML, and misses.csv. Zotero is the one that is well tested. Mendeley and EndNote adapters are seeking testers.

The miss list is the product. A miss is one of these: no_doi, paywalled, no_oa, fetch_failed, license_blocked.

PDFs are an add-on, and both of these have to be true. You already have somewhere to put the files — a zip you will store, or a WebDAV URL you already have. And the license lets you keep a copy. Open access is not permission for me to hand the file over. If the terms do not allow the copy, you get the link and fetch it yourself. Any file that leaves keeps its provenance (paperful oa:…). I will not put a Sci-Hub stamp on anything I send you.

You keep Zotero. You hold the disk. I run the commands and hand over a zip or a folder. I do not host your library, your PDFs, or a Zotero server.

For a job I do for someone else, the sources are open-access indexes only: Unpaywall, OpenAlex, arXiv, bioRxiv/medRxiv, Europe PMC, Semantic Scholar, CORE, and the item’s own URL. Campus access is your library card, on your machine. Grey literature and policy packs are not part of this.

A full Zotero quota is a normal result. Citation data sync is already free. The quota is attachments: 300 MB free, then paid Zotero storage. WebDAV is for a personal library only. Group libraries cannot use WebDAV. Files on WebDAV are not the same as files in the zotero.org web library. Zotero will not support a third-party WebDAV server.

If a grant line needs a receipt, say so in the reply.
